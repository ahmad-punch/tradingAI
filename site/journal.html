Trade journal

Every trade, every rule, every week. The page checks the numbers the same way the Python tools in tools/ do.

What this page is for. Before each trade you tick a short checklist. After it closes you write down the result. The page then works out whether the plan is actually working, and whether you would have broken the trading firm's exam rules.

R means "one unit of risk". If you risk $100 and make $250, that trade is +2.5R. If you lose the $100, it is −1R. Using R instead of dollars lets us compare trades of different sizes fairly.

Journal, position sizing, statistics, prop-rule replay, weekly review and post-mortems. All figures are computed in assets/calc.js, a line-for-line port of tools/journal_stats.py (load/stats), tools/rule_guard.py (parse/replay) and tools/position_size.py. Trades are converted to TRADE-LOG.csv rows first, then fed through the ported loaders, so the numbers here equal what the Python tools print on the exported CSV.

R = result ÷ initial risk. Percent figures derived from R use one risk % (the setting below), as journal_stats.py --risk-pct does; the rule guard uses each trade's own risk_pct.

Position size calculator

You never choose how big a trade is. You decide how much you are willing to lose (for example 0.75% of the account), you decide where the stop loss goes based on the chart, and the size falls out of the arithmetic. That way a bad trade always costs the same small, planned amount, no matter how confident you felt.

lots = (balance × risk%) ÷ (SL pips × pip value per lot), with contract size and pip size from the SPECS table in position_size.py. USD account assumed; for a pair whose quote currency is not USD, enter the quote→USD rate (e.g. JPY ≈ 1/150 ≈ 0.0067; GBP ≈ 1.27 for EURGBP).

Pre-trade checklist and new trade

Tick each box only if it is honestly true. If any box is empty, the trade breaks the plan. You can still record it (recording it is better than hiding it), but it will count against your rule-following score.

All seven must be true for rules_followed = Y. Any unticked item forces N after a confirm. Items mirror docs/04 (bias, location, trigger, SL, sizing, news filter, correlation / max exposure).

MAE = how far the trade went against you at its worst, in R. Example: risked $100, it was down $40 at the worst moment, then won: MAE is 0.4. Leave it blank if you don't know; the rule guard then assumes the worst (a full 1R for losers).

Risk defaults to the setting, 0.5% for XAUUSD (house rule risk_per_trade_pct_gold). Use 0.5% in the first month on any account. Blank MAE → rule guard assumes 1.0R for losers, 0.5R for winners.

Statistics and the Phase 2 gate

These numbers answer one question: if you keep trading exactly like this, do you make or lose money over many trades? The most important one is rule adherence (did you follow the plan?), then expectancy (what an average trade earns, in R).

Port of journal_stats.stats(). Window = trades closed on or after the date shown (--since); "%" figures are R × the risk % setting.

Phase 2 gate (last 90 days, always)

The gate is the pass mark for moving from free practice to paying for a real exam. It is measured over the last three months, whatever window you picked on the left.

Rule guard

This is the firm's exam rules, checked automatically every time you log a trade, so you find out here instead of on a real $200 fee. It replays your trades day by day and asks: on any day, did you lose more than the daily limit, or fall below the overall loss line?

Port of rule_guard.replay() with its conservative model: realised P&L booked on the close date; each trade's worst floating loss (MAE × risk) assumed on every day it is open; daily loss measured from the day-start balance against initial balance; max loss static unless drawdown_type is trailing. Trades without a result (still open) are skipped, as in parse(), so a live open position is not in this replay.

Backtest log and the Phase 1 gate

Before risking anything, you replay old charts and record what the plan would have done, 100 times. If the plan doesn't make money on old charts, it won't on new ones.

Same stats() function; % figures at the house 0.75% risk, as the Phase 1 gate in docs/03 specifies. Export writes the journal/BACKTEST-LOG.csv header.

Weekly review

Every Sunday, 20 minutes. Look at the numbers, write down every rule you broke, and pick one thing to do differently. One thing, about how you trade, not about how much you make.

From journal/WEEKLY-REVIEW-TEMPLATE.md. Numbers are a frozen snapshot of stats() (7d / 30d / stage filter) plus the rule-guard result at save time.

Post-mortems

When an account is blown (for real or in the rule guard), a month hits −4%, or a gate is failed: stop, wait 48 hours, then write this before any new trade. The key question is whether you lost while following the rules (bad luck, carry on) or because you broke them (go back a phase for 60 days).
