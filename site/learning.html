    Explicitly skipped (for now)

    Things that look like shortcuts and are not. You will meet these online. We skip them on purpose, for now.

    • Trading styles with fancy names (ICT, SMC, Elliott Wave, harmonic patterns). The rules are loose. Two people can look at the same chart and disagree. If two people cannot agree, we cannot test it, and a computer cannot check it.
    • Lots of chart add-ons (indicators). We use only two simple ones to start. More of them mostly means more ways to fool yourself.
    • Any paid course. The lessons we need are free. See "Why free material first" below.
    • Any Telegram or Discord room that sends trade tips. Never follow someone else's tips with your money. This is rule number one.
    • Crypto. The person in the video lost money this way. Prop-firm rules for it also often differ.

    We can look at any of these again later, but only by writing down why, in the decision log.

    Psychology: how people lose, and how we avoid it

    Everything the person in the video did wrong has a name, and it is predictable. We do not try to be stronger than him. We set things up so each mistake is hard to make.

    1. Chasing a loss

    Putting in more money, or trading harder, to win back what you lost. This is how a small loss becomes a big one.

    How we avoid it

    A fixed budget that is agreed in advance, kept separate, never topped up mid-year. After any breach, 48 hours with no trading and a written note about what happened.

    2. Trusting someone else's tips

    Paying for a group that says "buy this now, it will double today". Greed and hope do the buying, not you.

    How we avoid it

    No tips, ever. Anyone promising a quick double is a red flag, not an opportunity. Every trade has a stop (an exit for a small loss) set before you enter.

    3. Changing the plan after a bad week

    Switching to faster, riskier trading, or trying a new method on a paid test. The person in the video blew about 15 accounts like this.

    How we avoid it

    One written strategy. All experimenting happens free, in practice mode. Changing the plan needs a written decision, not a bad night. A weekly loss limit stops trading for the week.

    4. Feeling like a genius after wins

    Two good months, then a big bet, then it is all gone.

    How we avoid it

    After a good month, we risk less, not more. After a big win, 24 hours off. The monthly goal (3%) is a ceiling, not a minimum.

    5. Doing it alone, and watching all day

    No one checks on you, and you stare at charts instead of living. In the story his grades fell while he watched trades.

    How we avoid it

    Slow, daily-chart trading, checked at most twice a day. A weekly review shared with one person you trust. Do not talk about open trades with anyone.

    From docs/06-psychology.md. The aim is to make each failure structurally hard to commit.

    Before every trade: the checklist

    Take 60 seconds. Answer yes or no to each. If any answer is "no", you do not trade.

    1. Do I know which direction the bigger picture points (the "bias")?
    2. Is the price at a place my rules say is worth looking at (the "location")?
    3. Did my rule's signal actually happen (the "trigger"), or am I hoping?
    4. Have I set my safety exit (stop loss)?
    5. Did I calculate the trade size with the tool, not by feel?
    6. Did I check the news calendar for today and tomorrow?
    7. Am I already exposed to the same thing in another trade (correlated exposure)?

    After the trade closes, and before looking at profit: score yourself 0 to 10 on how well you followed your own rules. That score matters more than the money.

    Pre-trade checklist (journal row): bias? location? trigger? SL set? size computed? news checked? correlated exposure? Any "no" means no trade. Post-trade, before viewing P&L: rate rule adherence 0-10; adherence is the KPI (gate: at least 95% by Phase 2 exit).

    After any breach (simulated or real): 48 hours of no trading and a post-mortem (journal/POST-MORTEMS.md). After any big win: 24 hours off and re-read the overconfidence row.

    Sunday ritual (30 minutes)

    1. Sit down once a week, on Sunday. Set a timer for 30 minutes.
    2. Fill in the weekly review page: the numbers, the rules you broke (even small ones), trades you skipped that were fine, and one thing you will do differently next week. It must be about how you act, not about winning.
    3. Send it to your partner. They do not have to understand trading. They are there so you cannot quietly skip this.
