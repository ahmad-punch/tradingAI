OD-6: is it legal and tax-safe for you to do this from Pakistan? Settle this before you pay anything.

In May 2022 the State Bank of Pakistan told banks to block payments to overseas forex-trading sites, and said residents trading forex abroad breaks the foreign-exchange law. Prop-firm fees are a different thing, because you pay a fee for a pretend (simulated) test and no trading money leaves the country, and these firms do accept Pakistani clients. But it is a grey area. Pakistani cards often fail at their checkouts, and the usual workaround (paying in USDT through peer-to-peer sellers) has its own legal risk. Payouts are also foreign income, so tax matters too.

What to do: one hour with a Pakistani lawyer or accountant, before the first paid fee, and a written summary. Keep every payout invoice.

OD-6: legal/tax position in Pakistan. Decide before any paid fee.

A May 2022 State Bank of Pakistan circular directed banks to block payments to offshore forex trading platforms and characterised resident forex trading abroad as a violation of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. Prop-firm fees are a different thing (a fee for a simulated service, no trading capital sent abroad), and the firms in question accept Pakistani clients, but it is a grey area, PKR cards usually fail at these checkouts, and the common workaround (USDT via peer-to-peer) has its own compliance exposure.

Tax: payouts are foreign-source income. Keep every payout invoice. Ask an accountant how prop-firm income is treated for a Pakistan resident before the first payout, not after. Resolution: a one-hour consultation with a lawyer or accountant familiar with FERA/SBP circulars and foreign-source income; written summary in research/legal.md. This also feeds the D-006 gate.
