Money

Every dollar in and out, the $600 envelope, the stop rule and the payout rule.

The envelope

Think of a real envelope with $600 in it. Exam fees for trading firms come out of that envelope and nothing else, for the first 12 months. When it's empty, we stop paying for exams until next year, or until money the trading itself earned can pay. It is never topped up from salary.

Decision D-004: maximum $600 on challenge fees in the first 12 months from the first paid fee; separate envelope; no mid-year top-ups; maximum two paid attempts inside it. A ledger row counts as a challenge fee when its item contains the word "challenge" and it has an Out amount. Other outgoings (tooling) are capped separately at $20/month (docs/05).

Payout rule

When a payout arrives, half of it goes to the household straight away, every time, until the other halves have built up a $2,000 trading pot. That pot, not salary, pays for any future exams.

D-004 / docs/05: the first 50% of every payout goes to the household, never back into trading, until the bankroll of payouts reaches $2,000. Computed from ledger rows with an In amount (fee refunds included), in date order. Once the bankroll reaches $2,000 the split rule has done its job; anything after that is shown as "after the cap" and should be allocated by a decision record.

Working shortlist (open decision OD-1): FTMO Swing 2-step first, The5ers High Stakes as backup, FundedNext for diversification later. Never more than 40% of allocation at one firm once scaling: firms do close.

Legal check first. Pakistan's central bank (the State Bank) told banks in 2022 not to process payments to offshore forex trading platforms, and treats residents trading forex abroad as breaking foreign-exchange law. Paying a firm for a pretend-money exam is probably different, because no trading money leaves the country, but nobody has confirmed that for us. So before the first fee is paid, we spend an hour with a Pakistani lawyer or accountant. Payouts are also income that may need declaring; keep every receipt.

Legal note (open decision OD-6). A 2022 State Bank of Pakistan circular bars banks from processing payments to offshore forex trading platforms and treats resident forex trading abroad as a FERA violation. Prop-firm fees are a different posture (a fee for a simulated service; no trading capital remitted) but it is a grey area, not something to assume. PKR cards usually fail at checkout; USDT via P2P carries its own compliance risk. OD-6: a one-hour consultation with a Pakistani lawyer/accountant, written up in research/legal.md, before any paid fee. Payouts are foreign-source income: keep every invoice and payout record.

What could this become?

A firm lets you trade a pretend account of a certain size (the "allocation") and pays you a share of the profit. This little calculator shows what a month could look like. It is a way to sense the scale, not a promise or a plan.

Gross = allocation × monthly %; net = gross × your split. Monthly % is clamped to 0-3% (the house monthly target is 3% and the roadmap models 1.25%). Not saved.

Real months are not average months. There will be losing months that pay nothing. To earn $50,000 a month you would need about $4 million of firm money, which almost no firm gives one person, and it would take years. A realistic good outcome is $2,000-10,000 a month after 2-3 years, and most people who try never reach a first payout.

Losing months exist and pay $0; the figure is an average, not a monthly income. $50k/month needs roughly a $4M allocation at 1.5%/month, beyond most firms' per-trader caps ($1-4M) and a 4-6 year, low-probability outcome, not a plan. $2-10k/month over 2-3 years is the realistic success scenario (docs/03, Phase 5).
